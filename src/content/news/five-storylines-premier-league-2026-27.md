## Arsenal defend the title

Arsenal go into the new season as champions for the first time in over two decades, having finished last season seven points clear of Manchester City. Holding onto the title brings a different kind of pressure to chasing one, and how Arsenal handle the extra scrutiny — and a longer fixture list with Champions League football — will be one of the defining stories of the campaign.

## Three new faces in the top flight

Coventry City, Ipswich Town and Hull City arrive in the Premier League after securing promotion, replacing West Ham United, Burnley and Wolverhampton Wanderers. Each promoted side will be judged on survival first, with recruitment over the summer likely to determine how competitive they are from matchday one.

## A World Cup summer's ripple effects

With the 2026 World Cup wrapped up shortly before pre-season, several Premier League squads have had to manage players returning late from international duty — Spain's triumph over Argentina in the final meant an especially short turnaround for some. Managers will be balancing freshness against match sharpness in the opening weeks.

## The market keeps moving

Big-name departures from Chelsea, Newcastle and elsewhere show how active this transfer window has been, and further business is expected before it closes on 1 September. Clubs still searching for a striker or a settled back four will be watched closely deadline day approaches.

## Champions League football returns to more grounds

With five Premier League clubs qualifying for the Champions League from last season's standings, the division's European workload is heavier than usual heading into 2026/27 — a factor that could shape squad rotation and title-race fatigue as the season wears on.
