Fulham have entered advanced talks to appoint Alvaro Arbeloa as their next head coach, with the Spaniard lined up to replace the departing Marco Silva at Craven Cottage. The move would end Silva's lengthy spell in charge of the club and mark the start of a new era on the touchline for the Cottagers.

Arbeloa, a European Cup winner as a player with Real Madrid and part of Spain's 2010 World Cup-winning squad, has built his coaching reputation working through the youth and B-team ranks since retiring, and this appointment would represent his first major first-team job outside his homeland. Fulham's hierarchy have reportedly been impressed by his tactical ideas and his track record of developing young players, qualities the club is keen to embed into its first-team squad.

Silva's exit brings to a close a period in which he steadied Fulham in the Premier League and oversaw some of the club's more consistent top-flight seasons in recent memory. Supporters will now watch closely to see how quickly a formal announcement follows and what backing the incoming coach receives in the transfer market before the new season begins.

Fulham are expected to move swiftly to finalise terms, with the club keen to give any new head coach as much time as possible on the training ground before pre-season fixtures start in earnest.
