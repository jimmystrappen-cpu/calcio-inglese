Ipswich Town have announced that Kieran McKenna has decided to resign as head coach, ending his spell in charge just as the club prepares for a new Premier League season. The decision brings a close to a period widely regarded as one of the most successful in the club's recent history, having overseen back-to-back promotions that took Ipswich from League One into the top flight.

McKenna's departure comes at a sensitive moment for the club, with pre-season preparations already under way and a Premier League campaign on the horizon. His exit means Ipswich's hierarchy must now move quickly to identify a replacement capable of building on the foundations he leaves behind, while also managing any uncertainty the change may create among players and supporters alike.

Under McKenna, Ipswich developed a reputation for attacking, front-footed football that helped them punch above their weight against sides with considerably larger resources. His work at Portman Road elevated his profile significantly within English football, and his exit is likely to prompt fresh speculation about where he might land next.

The club have not immediately confirmed a timeline for appointing a successor, but with the new season approaching, Ipswich will be keen to have a new head coach in place as soon as possible. Supporters will now wait to see who the board turns to in order to continue the momentum built over recent campaigns.
