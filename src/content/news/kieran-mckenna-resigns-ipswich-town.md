Ipswich Town have announced that Kieran McKenna has decided to resign as head coach, a decision that lands just weeks before the club's return to the Premier League. The news brings an end to a hugely successful chapter at Portman Road that saw McKenna transform the club's fortunes.

McKenna arrived at Ipswich during a difficult period and oversaw back-to-back promotions that carried the Tractor Boys from League One into the Premier League, a rise that few could have predicted when he first took charge. His work on the training ground and his development of a settled, cohesive squad were widely credited as central to that rapid climb up the divisions.

His departure now leaves Ipswich searching for a replacement at a sensitive moment, with pre-season preparations for a fresh Premier League campaign already under way. The club will be keen to move quickly to appoint a successor who can build on the foundations McKenna leaves behind without disrupting the momentum built over the past two seasons.

Tributes to McKenna's spell in charge have focused on the scale of what he achieved with limited resources, with many inside the club regarding him as one of the most significant figures in Ipswich's modern history.
